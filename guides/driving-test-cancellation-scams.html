Driving test cancellation scams — what to watch for

If someone is offering to sell you a driving test date, it isn't a real DVSA booking. Here's how the scam works and what to do if you've been targeted.

The one fact that ends most of these scams

A DVSA practical test booking is tied to the learner who booked it — their name, licence number and theory pass certificate. It can only be changed or swapped by DVSA itself, over the phone, with both learners confirming it directly. Nobody else can hand you a test date, sell you one, or "release" one to you for a fee. If a date changes hands for money outside that DVSA phone call, whatever you've bought isn't a genuine booking.

How the scam usually works

  • Fake availability. A social media account, bot account, or website claims to have found you an earlier test and asks for payment upfront "to secure it."
  • Licence-detail phishing. You're asked for your driving licence number, theory pass certificate number, date of birth and card details on a site that isn't gov.uk — sold as necessary to "book on your behalf."
  • Automated bots. Some services run software that hammers the official cancellation checker faster than a person can and resell the slots it finds, charging learners who are desperate to move their date. Since May 2026, DVSA has explicitly banned this kind of third-party booking activity — only the learner can book or manage their own test.
  • Take the money, vanish. In the worst cases, no test ever existed. You've simply paid a stranger and received nothing.

Red flags

  • Anyone asking for payment to "get you" or "hold" a test date.
  • Pressure to act immediately ("only available for the next 10 minutes").
  • A request for your full licence number and theory pass number before you've agreed anything.
  • Contact through unofficial channels — random DMs, WhatsApp groups, Facebook Marketplace listings for "driving test slots".
  • A website that isn't gov.uk asking you to log in with your driving licence details.

How to report a scam

  1. Action Fraud — the UK's national reporting centre for fraud and cyber crime: actionfraud.police.uk or 0300 123 2040.
  2. DVSA — report unofficial booking services or suspected fraud directly to DVSA via gov.uk/contact-dvsa.
  3. The platform it happened on — report the account or listing to Facebook, Instagram, TikTok or WhatsApp so others don't fall for the same one.
  4. If you've shared card details, contact your bank immediately to flag the transaction.
On TestNow specifically: if anyone in a TestNow chat asks you for money, a phone number, or your licence details outside the DVSA call itself, use the Report button in the chat. We remove contact details automatically and investigate every report.

Questions

Can someone legally sell me their driving test slot?

No. A DVSA test booking belongs to the learner who booked it and can only be moved by DVSA itself, with both people confirming by phone. Any exchange of money for a 'slot' outside that process is not a real transfer and is very likely a scam.

Is it a scam if a website says it can book me an earlier test for a fee?

Since DVSA's May 2026 rules, only the learner can book or manage their own driving test — third-party booking services are explicitly against DVSA's terms. A paid site offering to do this for you is, at minimum, breaking those rules, and often has no real access to your booking at all.

I've already paid someone for a test date — what should I do?

Contact your bank straightaway to see if the payment can be recovered or disputed, then report it to Action Fraud (actionfraud.police.uk or 0300 123 2040) and to DVSA via gov.uk/contact-dvsa.
