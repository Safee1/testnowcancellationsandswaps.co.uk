You've passed. What happens next

Congratulations — that's the hard bit done. Here's what actually matters in the first week, written for someone who has never insured or owned a car before.

1. Your licence

If you handed your provisional to the examiner, DVLA sends your full licence by post, usually within about three weeks. If you kept your provisional, you apply to DVLA yourself. Either way you can drive straight away — your pass certificate number is the proof, and insurers will ask for it.

Keep the pass certificate safe. If it goes missing before your licence arrives, getting insured gets awkward.

2. Don't drive until the car is insured for you

This catches people out on day one. A learner policy normally ends the moment you pass, and being a named driver on a parent's policy as a learner does not automatically continue afterwards. Driving uninsured is six penalty points and a fine, and with the New Drivers Act that alone can cost you the licence you just earned.

Before you drive away, check one of these is true:
  • You have your own policy on the car, starting today.
  • You are a named driver on someone else's policy as a full licence holder, and they've told the insurer you've passed.
  • You have temporary cover (an hour, a day, a week) on that specific car.

Third party only is not automatically cheaper for new drivers. Always price comprehensive as well before deciding.

4. What actually moves the price

  • The car. Insurance group matters more than almost anything. A group 1–5 car can cost less to insure than the same driver in a group 20 car by thousands.
  • Where it sleeps. A driveway or garage usually beats the street.
  • Mileage. Estimate honestly. Guessing low to get a cheaper quote can void a claim.
  • Who else is on the policy. Adding an experienced driver can lower it. Making them the main driver when they aren't is fronting, and insurers check.
  • Paying monthly. It's a credit agreement with interest. Annual is cheaper if you can manage it.
  • Excess. A higher voluntary excess lowers the premium, but you have to be able to find that money after a bump.

5. Worth doing in the first month

  • Pass Plus — extra training covering motorways and night driving. Some insurers discount for it, so check whether yours does before paying.
  • Motorways — you can drive on them alone now. If you've never been on one, take an experienced passenger the first time.
  • Stay under 6 points for two years. Reaching 6 revokes your licence and you start again with both tests.
